2026

  • Understanding Information-Seeking Behavior in Collaborative Environments
    Your Name, Jane A. Doe, and John B. Smith
    Proceedings of the ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI), 2026 — Best Paper Honorable Mention
    pdf acm dl code website
    This paper investigates how individuals seek and share information within collaborative digital workspaces. Through a mixed-methods study involving 45 participants across three organizations, we identify key patterns in information-seeking behavior and propose a framework for designing more effective collaborative tools. Our findings suggest that contextual awareness and lightweight notification systems significantly improve team information flow. We discuss design implications for enterprise productivity software and future directions for studying hybrid remote work environments.
  • Visual Sensemaking in Data-Rich Dashboards: An Eye-Tracking Study
    Alice C. Johnson, Your Name, and Mark D. Lee
    Proceedings of the ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI), 2026
    pdf acm dl arxiv
    Data dashboards are ubiquitous in organizational settings, yet little is known about how users visually navigate complex, multi-panel displays. Using eye-tracking with 30 participants, we study gaze patterns, fixation durations, and saccadic sequences when interpreting business intelligence dashboards. We find that information architecture and color encoding significantly guide attention and affect decision quality. We contribute design guidelines for reducing cognitive load in high-information displays.

2025

  • Designing for Trust: A Study of Explainable AI in High-Stakes Decision Making
    Jane A. Doe, Your Name, and Alice C. Johnson
    ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction (TOCHI), 2025
    pdf acm dl
    Explainable AI (XAI) systems are increasingly deployed in domains such as healthcare and criminal justice. We report on a series of design probes and interviews examining how clinicians and case workers interpret and act upon AI-generated explanations. Participants often relied on heuristics rather than system outputs, highlighting the importance of calibrated trust and domain-sensitive explanation design.
  • Algorithmic Curation and Its Effect on Online Community Cohesion
    Your Name and Robert E. Williams
    Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference (WWW), 2025
    pdf website arxiv code & data
    Content recommendation algorithms shape the information diet of online community members. Using longitudinal trace data from three online platforms, we examine how algorithmic curation affects community cohesion, participation patterns, and the spread of shared norms. We find that high-exposure algorithmic recommendation correlates with reduced cross-community interaction but increased within-community engagement.
  • Privacy Expectations in Smart Home Ecosystems: A Diary Study
    Your Name, Susan F. Green, and David H. Park
    Proceedings of the ACM on Interactive, Mobile, Wearable and Ubiquitous Technologies (IMWUT), 2025
    pdf acm dl
    Smart home devices collect rich streams of behavioral data, yet users often have limited awareness of what is recorded and shared. We conducted a four-week diary study with 24 households, collecting daily reflections on privacy attitudes, disclosure events, and system interactions. Participants described layered privacy expectations tied to device type, household composition, and perceived data utility.

    Your Name
    Workshop on Auditing and Accountability in AI, CSCW 2024
    pdf
    This workshop position paper argues for including affected communities in the design and execution of algorithmic audits.
